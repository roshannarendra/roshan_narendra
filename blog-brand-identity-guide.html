Brand Identity Design: What You Get and How to Brief a Studio

More than a logo

A brand identity is the set of visual and verbal decisions that make your business recognisable: the logo, colours, typography, imagery style and the rules for using them. A logo alone looks fine on a business card, but a full identity keeps your website, packaging, social posts and ads looking like they come from the same company.

1. What a brand identity usually includes

Logo and variations. A primary logo plus versions for dark backgrounds, small sizes and square social avatars.

Colour palette. A small set of primary and supporting colours with exact values for screen and print.

Typography. Heading and body fonts, with simple rules for sizes and spacing.

Imagery and graphic style. The look of photos, renders, icons and illustrations so new content stays consistent.

Brand guidelines. A short document that shows how to use all of the above, so anyone on your team or any freelancer can follow it.

2. How the process works

Most studios follow similar steps: a short discovery to understand your business and audience, a round of directions to react to, refinement of the chosen direction, then delivery of final files and guidelines. Agreeing the number of concept rounds and revisions up front keeps the timeline and cost predictable.

3. How to brief a studio

A clear brief saves time and money. Share what you sell and who you sell to, three to five brands you like (and why), anything you want to avoid, where the brand will appear first (website, packaging, social, print) and any deadlines. If you already have a logo or assets, say which parts must stay.

4. Think about where it will be used

Ask for files in the formats you need: vector logos for print, optimised versions for web, and templates for social posts. If you plan motion, video or 3D content, mention it early so the identity works in animation and on screen as well as on paper.

5. Making it last

A good identity should still work in a few years. Keep the system simple, choose colours and fonts that stay legible, and keep the guidelines somewhere your whole team can find them. If you need help extending the identity into ads, store design or product visuals, a studio that also handles 3D, motion and web can keep everything consistent.
